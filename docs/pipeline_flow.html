How one company becomes 41 sourced data points, plus executive pay per role

Each company goes through seven stages: free official sources first, then the annual report, then the remuneration report, then other company documents for whatever is still missing. Every value keeps the exact place it came from: an API URL, an XBRL tag, a PDF page, or a web page. The fields follow the client's source-validation protocol: company grain C01–C23, role grain R01–R11. Open any box marked "+" for the detail.

Input row

Identify the company

GLEIF: name, ISIN or register number → LEI
APIfree
How the match works
  • An LEI in the input is used as given. Otherwise the name must match GLEIF's legal name, ignoring case, accents and how the legal form is written (AB = Aktiebolag, "A S" = "A/S" = "AS", Oyj = plc).
  • It must be registered in the expected country. If several companies match, none is used: the code never guesses.
  • No name match: GLEIF is searched by the ISIN or the national register number, when the input has them.
  • The LEI is the key for most of what follows: ESEF filings, FIRDS, the parent company, and (through GLEIF's registeredAs) the national registers.

Why APIs first, and what they can't do
  • A register field is exact (confidence A) and costs nothing, so it beats anything read from a document. Documents still fill everything the APIs leave empty.
  • A failing API is logged and skipped: one being down never stops a company.
  • Run order: in the code, the annual-report search (stage 3, process_company) runs first, because every company needs the report anyway (no API gives revenue, employees, owners or pay) and the API step uses the filing's CIK and register number. The APIs then run before any AI reads the report, and the AI is asked only for what they left open.
  • Not connected: Sweden (Bolagsverket) and Denmark (CVR) need credentials; parked, because for listed companies they add little.
  • Register industry codes often describe the listed holding company, not the business (Shell, AO World: SIC 70100 "activities of head offices").

What "recent" means, and which one wins
  • Recent = covers last year or later (FY2025 or newer during 2026). An older filing is kept; if the website has nothing newer, the older filing is used after all.
  • ESEF: the newest filing whose tagged figures cover a full 12 months. Filed under the company's own LEI, so its identity is certain.
  • Revenue and total assets come from XBRL tags when the filing has them (no AI). When a filing tags both "revenue" and "revenue and other operating income", IFRS revenue is used and the other goes in note (a client decision still pending).
  • A filing in Norwegian or Swedish still gives the tagged figures; the AI reads the text in that language and quotes it as printed.
  • An annual report URL given in the input skips the search.
Guards against the wrong report
  • Wrong year: years written as dates (31122025) and fiscal-year prefixes (fy25) are read; a newer year beats a better-looking link text (Rosenbauer, Jumbo).
  • A subsidiary's report: a file naming another legal form ("…plc" for an AG, "N.V." for a German company), or group-function words such as "treasury" or "finance", ranks lower (Siemens' Dutch financing arm, Volvo Treasury AB).
  • Identity check after reading: if the report's own text gives another country or another legal name, every value from it is graded C with the reason.
  • Rejected outright: quarterly reports, presentations, press releases, teaser pages (under 20,000 characters for an HTML report), reports over 3 years old, scanned PDFs (they would need OCR).

4

Data-point AI pass on the annual report

only the data points the APIs and XBRL tags left open
  1. The AI is asked only for what stages 2–3 left empty or graded C, plus the report's own legal name, country and ticker (for the identity check and the exchange's ticker spelling).
  2. The document is cut into ~1,500-word chunks, each remembering its PDF pages.
  3. Keywords (English, Nordic and other European languages) score each chunk for the topics of the open data points (of 7: ownership, employees, remuneration, geography, profile, segments, key figures).
  4. The best chunks per topic (~2,500 words each) go to Gemini 2.5 Flash, which must return an exact quote for every value, or no value. Output is forced into a fixed schema.
  5. The quote is searched in the document's own text: on the cited chunk's pages first, then everywhere. The page where it's found becomes …pdf#page=N.
PARSEno Firecrawl
5

Executive pay, one row per role

role grain R01–R11, plus company pay terms (C23)
Sources, tried in this order until one has pay for the right year
  1. A remuneration report URL from the input (skips the year check).
  2. SEC filings, when the company has a CIK: exhibits of the latest 20-F / 10-K labelled remuneration or compensation (Equinor's 20-F attaches its remuneration report), and the proxy statement (DEF 14A) for US filers. Free.
  3. UK and Ireland: the annual report's remuneration section first (UK reports carry the full pay tables).
  4. The remuneration report on the company's site. Second try: open the site's report-downloads page first, whose files join the pool (also files behind download buttons, read from the page's data), then a map search for "remuneration report".
  5. The annual report's remuneration section.
Pay tables in a PDF: read by Camelot, not by the AI

The pay AI call reads the whole document and gives every role with titles, targets and figures. Then, for the pages that look like pay tables (many numbers, several pay words; up to 6), extractor/pay_tables.py:

  1. Reads each table with Camelot (stream mode): the right columns, no AI. Prose read as columns (few figures) is dropped.
  2. Maps it with one small AI call that sees only the grid by row and column number, each column's header words, and the page text for the caption: which rows are this year's executives, and which columns make up which field (rules in prompts/pay_columns_system.md: other fees count as benefits, share savings plans as LTI, a salary addition in lieu of pension as pension). It gives numbers of rows and columns, never figures. Cached per table, so the same table maps the same way every run.
  3. Adds up the mapped cells of each row in code, with the table's unit.
  4. Checks that the parts add up to the table's own total (2%). A table is not used when it is for another year, has no total column, maps one column to two fields (a table with people as columns), or has no base salary.

Each checked row replaces the amounts of the role it belongs to (matched by name, title or base salary); titles, targets and maxima stay from the AI call. The quote is the person and the cells added ("Anders Opedal | 96 | 23"), graded B with the page; the note names the columns ("includes table: Other fees + Fringe benefits"). Anything not covered keeps the AI's reading. PAY_TABLES=0 turns it off.

Checks on the pay figures
  • Year check: a document whose pay is for an older year than the annual report is skipped (Shell's roadshow deck; Equinor's FY2024 report). Split years such as "2025/26" count by their end year.
  • Remuneration policy: read when targets or maxima are missing (STI/LTI as % of salary).
  • Aggregates: Swedish and Finnish remuneration reports cover the CEO only; the annual report's "other executives" total is added.
  • Derived values (method DERIVED): pension = pension + salary addition in lieu of pension; target as % of salary from "% of maximum" or "months of salary".
  • Total check: the parts must add up to the stated total within 2% (tried with vested LTI, granted LTI, or no LTI); a mismatch is noted.
  • Scaling guard: "1,242" in a "USD thousand" table becomes 1,242,000 in code; a value the AI already multiplied is caught by comparing it with the quote.

Document fallback: the company's other documents

only for company data points no earlier stage found
  1. Each missing data point has a list of document types likely to hold it: share page, shareholder page, governance report, sustainability report, legal notice, year-end report, listing notice, history, about page, AGM notice, report downloads page, remuneration report and policy.
  2. Candidate URLs come from the report search's lists first (free), then a plain map of the site, then a map search per type (1 credit each, each once).
  3. Up to 3 rounds; each round reads the best 3 documents and asks the AI only for the gaps. PDFs are downloaded for free; web pages cost 1 credit.

Pick the best value per data point

A before B before C, then API/XBRL before document before web page before derived

A different value from another source is kept in note for review. An ISIN read from a document and found in GLEIF's ISIN list becomes A. A ticker, exchange or ISIN implies "listed". The market-cap band is derived from shares × year-end price (EUR companies only, until year-end exchange rates are added). Questions and answers

How the mechanism works

How are we finding the company's website?
  1. Wikidata by LEI (free): items carry the LEI (P1278) and the official website (P856).
  2. Wikidata by name and country (free).
  3. Firecrawl web search (2 credits): "<legal name>" official website investor relations. Domains and titles are scored against the name; news sites, LinkedIn, Bloomberg and data portals are blocked. Below a score of 0.5, no website is used rather than a wrong one.
How are we finding documents on company websites?

Firecrawl's map returns the URLs it knows on a site for a search term (1 credit). We never crawl the whole site. For the annual report: "annual report", then the investor page's PDF links, then the native term ("årsrapport", "geschäftsbericht"…), then "integrated report" and "investors". For other documents, the URLs already found are reused first; a plain map, then a map search per document type, only when nothing fits.

Report-downloads pages (/reports, /downloads, /publications and local words) are read for file addresses kept in the page's data, which catches downloads behind buttons. File names say what a link is; when they say nothing (…/8769e063.pdf), the link text is judged instead.

How do we know the document belongs to the right company and year?
  • Filings (ESEF, SEC, registers) are found by the company's own identifier: identity is certain.
  • Website documents: ranking penalises other legal forms and group-function words; after reading, the identity check compares the report's own legal name and country with the company's, and grades everything C on a mismatch.
  • Year: pay documents for an older year are skipped; yearly figures from fallback documents not dated for the report's year are graded C.
How do we find the right part of a 300-page report?
  1. The report becomes text with one marker per PDF page, cut into chunks of up to ~1,500 words along headings, each remembering its pages.
  2. Each chunk is scored per topic with keyword lists in several languages; a keyword in a heading counts extra; statement tables get a boost for financials; tables of contents are skipped.
  3. The best chunks per topic go to the AI, about 2,500 words per topic, so it reads a fraction of the report.
How do you know which page had the value?
  1. The AI must quote: a passage under 25 words copied from one chunk, plus the chunk's number. No quote, no value.
  2. We search for the quote ourselves, after normalising both (case, table borders, thousands separators, quote marks).
  3. Cited chunk's pages first, then the whole document; exact match first, then a loose one (the words in order, a few extra words allowed).
  4. Found → #page=N and confidence B. Not found → confidence C, "quote not found verbatim".

The page is the PDF's own page number (what #page= opens), which can differ from the printed one. Filed XHTML has no pages, so those rows link with #:~:text=.

How do we stop the AI from inventing values?
  • It only sees document text, is told never to use outside knowledge, and must fill a fixed schema (structured output).
  • Every value needs an exact quote, which we verify; unverified ones are C.
  • Numbers are copied as printed with their unit; conversion is done in code, with a guard against values the AI already multiplied.
  • Exact sources (tags, registers) always beat AI-read values.
  • Classifications (owner type, PE stake) show their basis in note; computed values say DERIVED.
What if two sources disagree?

Confidence A before B before C, then API/XBRL before a filed document before a web page before a derived value. The other value is kept in note ("other source says …"), so differences are visible, not hidden. One exception: a ticker from OpenFIGI ("VOLVB") takes a document's spelling ("VOLV B") when the two agree.

What does an empty value mean?
  • not found in: GLEIF, ESMA FIRDS, …, Annual report (PDF), Share information (web page): every source listed was checked.
  • not applicable: …: listing fields for unlisted companies, cik for non-SEC registrants, pay for unlisted companies.
  • not derived: …: market-cap band for non-EUR companies.
  • For pay: one role row saying why ("no remuneration report found", "not found in: …").
What does it cost, and how do re-runs work?
  • Free: GLEIF, FIRDS, OpenFIGI, registers, SEC, ESEF, Wikidata, and every PDF download (converted locally).
  • Firecrawl, capped per company: up to 6 credits to find the annual report, up to 10 for pay and fallback documents (--doc-credits); --max-run-credits caps a whole run. Out of credits: the run continues on free sources and flags the company.
  • Gemini 2.5 Flash on Vertex AI for the AI calls (a few per company).
  • Caching: API answers for a week; documents, maps and AI answers (keyed by prompt and chunks) on disk, so a re-run with nothing changed costs nothing. Finished companies are skipped unless --redo.
Confidence

What each grade means

AExact

A register field or an XBRL tag, or an ISIN from a document confirmed in GLEIF's ISIN list. The endpoint is the API URL or the filing.

BRead by the AI, quote found

The quote was found in the source text, so the value can be checked on the linked page. Also: OpenFIGI tickers (Bloomberg spelling).

CCheck first

The quote wasn't found word for word; or the report failed the identity check; or a yearly figure came from a document not dated for the report's year; or a FIRDS listing date.

Known limits

What doesn't work yet (docs/known_issues.md)

Open
  • Pay tables not read by Camelot (issue 2): tables with people as columns (Nokia, Marimekko), tables without a total column (Bang & Olufsen) and pay given in prose still rely on the AI's reading of the converted text, which can vary between runs (issue 5).
  • Segments within a regulated market (issue 9): Large/Mid/Small Cap, Prime Standard need the exchanges' own files.
  • Holding companies (issues 6, 7): a UK holding company's own filed accounts have no group figures; register industry codes describe the holding company.
  • Report finder (issue 4): a placeholder page can still outrank a downloads page; a subsidiary's report with a plain file name isn't caught before reading.
  • Scanned PDFs are skipped (no OCR).
Waiting on the client
  • Definitions (issue 10): base salary with or without benefits; which revenue line; market-cap band thresholds.
  • The Allshares industry taxonomy, to classify sub-sector from the business description (issue 7).
  • Which fiscal year the golden files hold (issue 11).
